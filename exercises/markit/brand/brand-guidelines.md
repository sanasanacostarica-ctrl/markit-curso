# Planerio Brand Guidelines

> How we sound, what we stand for, and how every word should feel to a distributed team manager reading it at 4:47pm on a Thursday.

## 1. Brand voice

Planerio's voice is **professional but friendly, knowledgeable but not condescending**. We sound like a senior colleague who has actually managed a distributed team — someone who respects the reader's time, understands the problem deeply, and gets to the point.

Four attributes define the voice:

| Attribute | What it means | What it doesn't mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Professional** | Clear, precise, credible. We back claims with reasoning or data. | Stiff, corporate, detached. |
| **Friendly** | Warm, human, willing to use contractions and plain language. | Cute, over-familiar, emoji-heavy. |
| **Knowledgeable** | We understand distributed work, meeting overload, and maker schedules in depth. | Showing off, jargon dumps, acronym soup. |
| **Direct** | Benefits first. Short sentences. No filler. | Blunt, cold, or dismissive. |

**Perspective:** always **"we"** (Planerio) and **"you"** (the reader). Never "users" or "customers" in customer-facing copy.

## 2. Messaging pillars

Every piece of Planerio content should ladder up to one of these four pillars. Each pillar has a core claim, a proof point, and a typical audience.

### Pillar 1 — Coordinate, don't just schedule
- **Core claim:** Individual calendar optimization hits a ceiling. Teams win time back when the *team's* calendar is coordinated as a system.
- **Proof point:** AI negotiates focus blocks across the whole team, not just for one person.
- **Primary audience:** Manager Maria, Director Dana.

### Pillar 2 — Focus time is a team outcome
- **Core claim:** Deep work isn't a personal-discipline problem; it's a coordination problem. Protecting it is leadership's job.
- **Proof point:** Shared focus blocks, meeting-free windows, and team-wide norms enforced automatically.
- **Primary audience:** Manager Maria, Founder Fiona.

### Pillar 3 — Works where your team already works
- **Core claim:** Planerio fits into Slack, Zoom, and Google Workspace — no new tab, no new habit.
- **Proof point:** Native integrations with the three tools distributed teams already live in.
- **Primary audience:** All personas, especially IT-adjacent buyers.

### Pillar 4 — Predictable rhythms, happier teams
- **Core claim:** When collaboration windows are predictable, everything downstream gets easier: 1:1s, reviews, cross-team work, and hiring.
- **Proof point:** Team-level rituals (focus blocks, office hours, no-meeting days) become default, not exceptions.
- **Primary audience:** Manager Maria, Director Dana.

## 3. Tone guidelines

Voice is constant. **Tone shifts by context.** Here's how Planerio adjusts.

| Context | Tone | Example opening |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing homepage / ads** | Confident, benefit-led, a little aspirational. | "Give your team back four hours of focus this week." |
| **Blog / thought leadership** | Thoughtful, evidence-based, slightly conversational. | "Most productivity tools optimize the wrong unit: the individual." |
| **Product UI / in-app** | Calm, instructive, short. | "Planerio found 3 hours of overlapping focus time tomorrow." |
| **Onboarding emails** | Warm, encouraging, concrete. | "Welcome. Here's the fastest way to see Planerio work." |
| **Support & error states** | Direct, reassuring, never blaming. | "That didn't go through — here's what to try." |
| **Sales outreach** | Respectful, specific, short. | "Saw your team just grew past 50 — a quick thought." |

**Universal tone rules**
- Lead with the reader's benefit, not our feature.
- Prefer **active voice**. ("Planerio coordinates focus time" — not "focus time is coordinated by Planerio.")
- Short sentences. Break up walls of text.
- Contractions are welcome (*we're*, *you'll*, *it's*).
- One idea per sentence. One argument per paragraph.

## 4. Words to use — and words to avoid

### ✅ Use
- **coordinate / coordination** (our signature verb)
- **focus time**, **focus block**, **deep work**
- **distributed team**, **your team**
- **protect** (focus time), **negotiate** (calendars), **give back** (hours)
- **predictable**, **shared**, **team-wide**, **default**
- **rhythm**, **cadence**
- plain verbs: **help**, **show**, **find**, **plan**, **run**

### ⚠️ Use sparingly
- **productivity** — overused in the category; prefer *focus time* or *deep work* when possible.
- **AI** — only when it's meaningful to the claim; don't sprinkle it in for buzz.
- **remote** — acceptable, but **distributed** is more precise for our audience.

### ❌ Avoid
- **Hyperbole:** *revolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, world-class, next-generation, unleash, unlock*
- **Jargon & acronym soup:** *synergy, leverage (as a verb), paradigm, holistic, frictionless, seamless, robust, mission-critical, KPI-driven*
- **Condescending framing:** *simply, just, obviously, of course, as you know, don't worry*
- **Vague intensifiers:** *very, really, extremely, incredibly, truly*
- **Passive constructions:** *is enabled by, can be configured, is designed to*
- **Customer-speak about the reader:** *users, end-users, customers, individuals* — say **you** or **your team**.
- **Fear-based language:** *don't fall behind, before it's too late, the cost of inaction*

### Quick rewrites

| ❌ Don't write | ✅ Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Planerio leverages AI to unlock unprecedented productivity gains." | "Planerio coordinates focus time across your team — so everyone gets more of it." |
| "Our world-class platform empowers users to seamlessly optimize their workflows." | "Planerio finds shared focus blocks for your team and protects them automatically." |
| "Simply connect your calendar and let Planerio do the rest." | "Connect your calendar. Planerio takes it from there." |
| "Mission-critical focus time can be enabled via our robust scheduling engine." | "Planerio plans focus time for the whole team, not just one person." |

## 5. The 10-second gut check

Before any piece of Planerio content ships, read it aloud and ask:

1. Would a busy team manager read past the first sentence?
2. Is the benefit to **their team** obvious in the first two lines?
3. Did we say **coordinate**, not just *schedule* or *optimize*?
4. Are there any banned words? (Run a ctrl-F on the avoid list.)
5. Would we be embarrassed if a customer quoted this line back to us?

If any answer is *no*, rewrite.
